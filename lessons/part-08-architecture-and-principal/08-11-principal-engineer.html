The Principal Engineer: RFCs, ADRs, Mentoring & Strategy

🏛️ Principal · L7++ ⏱️ ~30 min 🧭 Beyond code

At the highest levels, your impact comes less from the code you personally write and more from the decisions you shape, the engineers you grow, and the technical direction you set. This lesson is about the non-code skills that define a distinguished principal engineer (L7++) — the ones that turn a great coder into a force multiplier for an entire organization.

1. Writing: RFCs & ADRs

📝
Concept · Decisions are written, not just made

An RFC (Request for Comments) proposes a significant change and invites feedback before building — context, options, trade-offs, a recommendation. An ADR (Architecture Decision Record) captures a decision and its rationale so future engineers understand why. Writing clearly is the highest-leverage skill at senior+: it scales your thinking across time and people, surfaces flaws early, and builds alignment.

  • A good RFC states the problem, success criteria, options with honest trade-offs, a recommendation, and risks — and is short enough to be read.
  • A good ADR records the context, the decision, the alternatives considered, and the consequences — in a few paragraphs, versioned in the repo.

2. Multiplying others

  • Code review as teaching: explain the why, suggest don't demand, praise good work. Reviews are the highest-bandwidth mentoring you do.
  • Mentoring & sponsoring: grow engineers deliberately; give them stretch work and visibility, not just advice.
  • Setting standards: the linters, the CI gates, the design system, the conventions — you make the right thing the easy thing for everyone.
  • Unblocking: a principal's calendar is full of removing obstacles for others; their output is the team's increased output.

3. Technical strategy & judgement

  • Align tech to business: the best technical choice is the one that serves the product and the team, not the most clever one.
  • Manage technical debt deliberately: know when to take it on and when to pay it down; make it visible.
  • Make reversible decisions quickly, irreversible ones carefully (one-way vs two-way doors).
  • Influence without authority: you lead through clarity, trust, and persuasion, not a title.
⚡
Pro-tip · The senior mindset shift

Junior: "How do I build this?" Senior: "Should we build this, and what's the simplest thing that solves the real problem?" Principal: "What problem should the org be solving, and how do I set up people and systems so the right outcomes happen without me?" Optimise for impact, not activity.

📝
Exercise · Write a real RFC

Pick a meaningful decision from one of your course projects (e.g. "Zustand vs Redux for app state", "Server Actions vs tRPC", "monorepo vs separate repos"). Write a one-page RFC: problem, options, trade-offs, recommendation, risks. Then write the matching one-paragraph ADR recording the decision. This is the artifact that distinguishes senior engineers.

🎓 Key takeaways

  • Writing (RFCs, ADRs) is the highest-leverage senior skill — it scales thinking and builds alignment.
  • Principals multiply others: teaching code reviews, mentoring, setting standards, unblocking.
  • Technical strategy aligns tech to business; manage debt deliberately; weigh reversibility.
  • Influence without authority — lead through clarity and trust, not a title.
